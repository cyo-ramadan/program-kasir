4. Commit the GitHub change.
5. Apply the same semantic change to the linked Google Drive document.
6. Verify version, headings, normative force, and content parity.

MAXI AI Engineering Constitution
Version 0.2 — ACTIVE
Approval bureaucracy status: INACTIVE by direct instruction of Bos Cyo. Reviewer or affected-owner approval is advisory and may not block starting, completing, merging, deploying, or reporting PASS. Technical gates remain ACTIVE: required tests, documentation, compatibility, security, data integrity, and migration/recovery. Production data mutation still requires explicit task authority from Bos Cyo.
Authority: Bos Cyo
Applies to every AI, developer, program, service, database, SDK, and integration in the MAXI ecosystem.


1. Purpose
This constitution preserves project continuity when an AI, model version, developer, framework, or system owner changes. Model knowledge is never treated as project memory. Project memory lives in versioned documentation, contracts, decisions, source code, and tests.


2. Normative Language
WAJIB means mandatory. DILARANG means prohibited. SEBAIKNYA means a deviation requires written reasoning. BOLEH means optional.


3. Non-Negotiable Rules
R1 — Every AI must read the required protocol and module context before changing code.
R2 — An AI must stop and ask when business meaning, ownership, contract, security, accounting, inventory, or migration behavior is ambiguous.
R3 — Programs communicate through approved APIs, events, and SDKs.
R4 — A program may not write directly to another program’s database.
R5 — Business applications report business facts. Accounting owns journal interpretation. Inventory owns stock and valuation interpretation.
R6 — Shared contracts are versioned and backward-compatible by default.
R7 — Posted journals and official stock movements are immutable. Corrections use reversal or adjustment with an audit trail.
R8 — Financial and inventory integrity decisions must be deterministic.
R9 — Every material change includes relevant documentation and tests in the same changeset.
R10 — Work is not complete without evidence, compatibility assessment, and handover.


4. Source of Truth
Business terminology belongs to the System Glossary.
Business invariants belong to Domain Rules.
API and event formats belong to OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, and JSON Schema.
Architecture rationale belongs to Architecture Decision Records.
Active module capability and limitation belong to Current State.
Implementation belongs to source code.
Compatibility evidence belongs to automated tests.
When two sources conflict, the AI must stop, identify the conflict, and request an owner decision. Silent alignment is prohibited.


5. Ownership and Approval — REVIEW/APPROVAL BUREAUCRACY INACTIVE
Every system and shared interface must have an owner.
Internal implementation changes may proceed under the active task instruction. Separate module-owner approval is INACTIVE.
Review from affected owners is ADVISORY and NON-BLOCKING. Shared contract, vocabulary, ownership, accounting, inventory, security, and breaking changes still require explicit impact, compatibility, test, and migration evidence.
Bos Cyo is the final decision maker when owners disagree.


6. Prohibited AI Behavior
An AI may not claim to have read inaccessible material.
An AI may not invent events, fields, statuses, account mappings, item mappings, tax behavior, timezone, or unit conversion.
An AI may not redesign unrelated code while completing a focused task.
An AI may not hide failures through silent fallback or fabricated defaults.
An AI may not declare completion while required tests fail or documentation is stale.
An AI may mark a shared rule active only when the active task contains direct authority from Bos Cyo; separate reviewer approval is INACTIVE.


7. Change Governance
Every change begins with an impact assessment.
Shared or breaking changes require an RFC, migration plan, compatibility plan, and test plan. Required reviewer/owner approval is INACTIVE.
Architecture decisions are recorded as new ADRs. Old ADRs remain visible and may be marked Superseded.
Protocol changes use semantic versioning.


8. Compliance Declaration
Before coding, the AI records the protocol version, project, contract versions, relevant interfaces, baseline test status, documentation impact, and ambiguities.
After coding, the AI records files changed, contracts changed, migrations, tests and results, backward compatibility, documentation updates, deployment notes, recovery plan, known issues, and open risks.
PASS means all mandatory gates are satisfied. FAIL or BLOCKED may not be reported as complete.


9. Ratification
Status: ACTIVE.
Approved by Bos Cyo. This document is ACTIVE and governs the MAXI ecosystem.
10. SaaS Multi-Tenant Product Direction
S1 — MAXI commercial programs are designed as multi-tenant SaaS products by default unless Bos Cyo explicitly approves another deployment model.
S2 — One deployed codebase may serve multiple customer companies, but every customer company is a separate tenant with strictly isolated identity, authorization, operational data, financial data, inventory data, configuration, subscription, and audit history.
S3 — Tenant context must be enforced server-side on every read, write, event, job, cache key, file, export, import, log, reconciliation, backup, and recovery path. Client-supplied tenant identity is untrusted until authorized.
S4 — Accounting, Inventory, Integration Bridge, and other shared services may serve many tenants from canonical domain services. They may not merge, expose, or process one tenant's business data as another tenant's data.
S5 — Cross-tenant access is prohibited except for an explicitly authorized platform operation with a documented purpose, least-privilege scope, immutable audit trail, and tests.
S6 — Architecture, database schemas, public contracts, naming, migrations, observability, testing, and recovery must be SaaS-ready before a program is declared production-ready.
S7 — Subscription plans and module entitlements control product access. Cancellation, suspension, retention, export, deletion, and reactivation behavior require explicit documented policies and tests; no AI may invent them.

Purpose: give every new or upgraded AI enough verified project context before it changes code.


1. When Full Onboarding Is Mandatory
Full onboarding is required for a new AI, new model version, new session, context reset or compaction, protocol version change, unfamiliar repository, shared contract change, and work involving architecture, security, accounting, inventory valuation, data ownership, or cross-system migration.


2. Mandatory Read Order
Step 1 — Read AI_GATE for non-negotiable rules.
Step 2 — Read the active Engineering Constitution version.
Step 3 — Read the module manifest and Current State.
Step 4 — Read relevant Glossary and Domain Rules entries.
Step 5 — Read relevant API, event, and schema contracts.
Step 6 — Read applicable ADRs, Known Issues, Known Pitfalls, and Runbook sections.
Step 7 — Inspect affected source code, tests, migrations, and uncommitted work.
Step 8 — Run baseline tests before editing.


3. Context Loading Levels
Level A — Quick Gate. Load for every coding task. Target 800–1,500 tokens. It contains protocol ID, version, hard rules, source-of-truth routing, prohibited behavior, and completion gate.
Level B — Task Context. Load only the module state, vocabulary, domain rules, contracts, ADRs, issues, code, and tests relevant to the requested change.
Level C — Full Governance Context. Load the complete protocol and relevant cross-domain documents for onboarding, contract work, architecture, security, accounting, valuation, ownership, migration, or documentation conflicts.
A tool call within the same verified session does not require re-reading the entire manual.


4. Context Budget
Reserve enough context for Quick Gate, task-specific documentation, relevant code and tests, tool output, reasoning, and final evidence.
Maintain at least 30 percent headroom for reasoning and response.
Do not fill the context window with unrelated documentation.
If important code or test evidence would be truncated, narrow the documentation retrieval before continuing.


5. Preflight Record
Before coding, record:
Change ID.
Project and module.
Requested change.
Protocol and contract versions.
Affected modules, APIs, events, database objects, and owners.
Contract impact: none, additive, or breaking.
Documentation impact: required or not required.
Security and migration impact.
Backward compatibility.
Required tests.
Open questions.
Ambiguity affecting behavior, data, security, accounting, inventory, or contracts blocks implementation until an owner decides.


6. Execution Rules
Use the existing architecture, SDK, naming, formatter, linter, and test framework.
Keep changes minimal and focused.
Preserve unrelated user or AI changes.
Do not invent missing facts.
Do not change shared contracts to simplify one local implementation.
Re-read live code and indexes after material or concurrent changes.


7. Completion Gate
A task is complete when required tests pass, documentation impact is resolved, compatibility is assessed, and migrations have an execution and recovery plan. Required reviewer approval is INACTIVE and advisory.

MAXI Documentation System & Module Memory Standard
Version 0.1 — Foundation Draft
Approval bureaucracy status: INACTIVE by direct instruction of Bos Cyo. Reviewer or affected-owner approval is advisory and may not block starting, completing, merging, deploying, or reporting PASS. 1. Documentation Architecture

Module documentation lives beside the source code of each program and is versioned in the same repository and changeset.
A central documentation hub may index module documents, but the module repository remains the canonical home for module-specific knowledge.


2. Required Module Documents
MODULE_MANIFEST records module ID, version, owner, protocol version, contracts, dependencies, events, and document paths.
MODULE_CONTEXT explains purpose, architecture, boundaries, workflow, and data ownership.
CURRENT_STATE contains active capabilities, limitations, pending migrations, supported versions, and current operational facts.
KNOWN_ISSUES contains unresolved defects.
KNOWN_PITFALLS contains repeatable traps and behavior that future maintainers must not accidentally reintroduce.
ERROR_CATALOG contains stable error codes, meaning, cause, retryability, and handling.
RUNBOOK contains operational diagnosis, recovery, reconciliation, and emergency actions.

ADR records why a material architecture or domain decision was made.


3. Documentation Impact Gate
Every change must declare DOC-IMPACT: REQUIRED or DOC-IMPACT: NOT_REQUIRED with an objective reason.
Documentation is required when a change affects an API, event, schema, enum, error code, business meaning, domain invariant, ownership, database schema, lifecycle, accounting, inventory, security, retry, idempotency, configuration, dependency, recovery, shared SDK, compatibility, architecture, limitation, or reusable behavior.
Formatting, private variable rename, and behavior-preserving local refactor may be Not Required.


4. Documentation Entry Control
Before writing, the AI must search for an existing canonical entry, classify the information, identify the document owner, and update the existing entry when the topic is the same.
Create a new entry only when no canonical home exists.
The Constitution may not become a feature diary.
Changelog may not define business rules.
Current State contains current facts, not historical narratives.
Examples may not override schemas.
The same rule may not be copied across multiple documents; use references.
Proposed, Accepted, Implemented, Deployed, Deprecated, Superseded, Rejected, and Unverified are distinct statuses.
An AI may mark a shared edit Active only under explicit Bos Cyo task authority. Separate owner/reviewer approval is INACTIVE.


5. Bug and Error Routing
An unresolved bug belongs in Known Issues.
A fixed trivial bug belongs in tests and Changelog.
A fixed bug with a repeatable lesson belongs in Known Pitfalls.
An operational failure with recovery steps belongs in Runbook.
A public or cross-program error belongs in the relevant Error Catalog or contract.
A bug exposing an architecture decision belongs in a new ADR.
Do not copy raw logs or large stack traces into durable manuals. Store stable symptoms, root cause, affected versions, resolution, regression test, evidence reference, and prohibited regression behavior.


6. Required Entry Metadata
Every material entry records:
Entry ID.
Status.
Owner.
Change ID.
Affected systems and versions.
Reason.
Source or evidence.
Contract impact.
Backward compatibility.
Migration or recovery when relevant.
Advisory reviews (non-blocking).


7. Atomic Documentation Rule
Code, contract, documentation, migration, and tests for one material behavior change belong in the same changeset.
Do not postpone mandatory documentation to a future task.
Do not describe a feature as active before it is implemented and deployed.
A fixed issue must be removed from Current Known Issues and routed to Changelog, Pitfalls, Runbook, or ADR as appropriate.


8. Module Documentation Read Order
A new AI reads AI_GATE, Module Manifest, Module Context, Current State, relevant Issues or Pitfalls, relevant contract, applicable ADR, and affected tests.
The AI reads only relevant entries unless a full onboarding trigger applies.


9. Documentation Quality Gate
Documentation must be specific enough for an unfamiliar AI to explain the module, identify its boundaries, locate the source of truth, recognize known hazards, and change it without violating contracts.

MAXI Integration Contract Standard
Version 0.2 — ACTIVE
Approval bureaucracy status: INACTIVE by direct instruction of Bos Cyo. Reviewer or affected-owner approval is advisory and may not block starting, completing, merging, deploying, or reporting PASS. Technical contract gates remain ACTIVE: approved schemas/events, required tests, compatibility, security, data integrity, and migration/recovery. Production data mutation still requires explicit task authority from Bos Cyo.
Purpose: make every business application connect consistently to Integration Bridge, Accounting, and Inventory.


1. Official Architecture
Business Application sends canonical business facts through the approved Business Core SDK.
Integration Bridge validates, deduplicates, maps, routes, records processing status, retries failures, and preserves audit references.
Accounting owns journals, receivables, payables, fiscal periods, closing, reversal, and accounting balances.
Inventory owns items, warehouses, stock movements, stock balances, BOM, costing, and valuation.


2. Application Responsibility
The application owns its operational transaction and decides when a business fact occurred.
It emits an approved event and stores a stable transaction ID, event ID, correlation ID, idempotency key, contract version, occurrence time, and business date.
It uses an outbox when local persistence and event delivery cannot be guaranteed atomically.
It records sync status and safely retries.
It does not determine accounts, journal lines, official stock valuation, or canonical warehouse balance.


3. Bridge Responsibility
The Bridge validates authentication, authorization, schema, producer registration, compatibility, mapping, and idempotency.
It routes one canonical event according to central rules.
It records Accepted, Processing, Completed, Needs Mapping, Failed Retryable, Failed Permanent, or equivalent approved statuses.
Missing mappings are surfaced. The Bridge may not guess.
The Bridge may not change event meaning based on source application.


4. Accounting and Inventory Boundary
A completed cash sale may produce revenue and payment entries in Accounting and stock issue plus valuation in Inventory.
The source application reports the sale. It never sends debit and credit account numbers.
Inventory produces the authoritative cost value used for inventory and cost-of-goods accounting.
Purchase order, goods receipt, supplier invoice, and supplier payment are distinct business events.
Posted records are corrected through reversal or adjustment.


5. Canonical Event Rules
Event names follow domain.past-tense-fact.vMajor, for example sale.completed.v1.
Events describe facts that already happened. Commands request actions and use a separate contract.
Every event defines producer, consumer, trigger condition, required fields, optional fields, semantic meaning, valid examples, invalid examples, retry behavior, idempotency behavior, error behavior, accounting implication, inventory implication, and compatibility policy.
An application may not introduce a private shared event without registering it centrally.


6. Data Standards
Money does not use binary floating point. IDR uses integer minor units according to contract.
Fractional quantity uses a decimal string and canonical unit of measure.
Integration timestamps use ISO 8601 UTC. Business date and branch timezone are explicit.
Null, empty string, empty array, and zero are different values.
External ID mapping is explicit and never inferred from similar names.
IDs from one application are not used as canonical IDs of another application without a registered mapping.


7. Reliability Rules
Duplicate delivery must create one business effect.
A retry reuses the same idempotency key.
Timeout does not prove failure.
The Bridge and every consumer keep an inbox or equivalent deduplication record.
Failed retryable processing uses controlled retry.
Permanent failures and exhausted retries enter a visible dead-letter or manual-resolution state.
Correlation ID links the source transaction, Bridge event, Accounting result, and Inventory result.
Reconciliation detects partial or missing processing.


8. Versioning
Patch clarifies without behavior change.
Minor adds backward-compatible capability.
Major contains breaking changes.
Removing, renaming, requiring a new field, changing a field meaning, or narrowing an enum is breaking.
Contracts and SDKs are pinned to explicit versions; latest is prohibited.
Deprecated versions require replacement, migration guide, support window, and retirement decision.


9. Mandatory Contract Tests

MAXI Coding & I/O Naming Standard
Version 1.1.0 — ACTIVE
Approval bureaucracy status: INACTIVE by direct instruction of Bos Cyo. Reviewer or affected-owner approval is advisory and may not block starting, completing, merging, deploying, or reporting PASS. Technical gates remain ACTIVE: required tests, documentation, compatibility, security, data integrity, and migration/recovery. Production data mutation still requires explicit task authority from Bos Cyo.
Authority: Bos Cyo
Scope: all source code, public contracts, SDKs, events, commands, APIs, adapters, and cross-program integrations in the MAXI ecosystem.


1. Purpose
This standard converts the active MAXI Constitution into concrete coding and lexical naming rules. It governs how code is changed and how public input/output names are formed. Domain meaning remains owned by approved glossaries, domain rules, schemas, contracts, source code, and tests.


2. Constitution Rule R2
The active Constitution R2 is the controlling Ambiguity Gate. This standard operationalizes R2 and does not duplicate, weaken, or replace it.
Coding must stop before implementation when business meaning, ownership, contract behavior, security, accounting interpretation, inventory interpretation, migration behavior, or compatibility is materially ambiguous.


3. Mandatory Coding Rules
C1 — Context First. Read the active AI_GATE, Module Manifest, Current State, relevant glossary and domain rules, applicable contracts and ADRs, Known Issues, Known Pitfalls, affected source code, migrations, and tests before editing.
C2 — Preflight First. Record scope, affected systems, owners, interfaces, database objects, contract impact, DOC-IMPACT, security, migration, compatibility, required tests, and unresolved questions before coding.
C3 — Focused Change. Make the smallest focused change that satisfies the approved request. Preserve unrelated user and AI work and do not redesign unrelated code.
C4 — Clear Domain Names. Use descriptive English domain identifiers. Vague names such as data, data1, abc, tmp, hasil, finalData, and processData are prohibited.
C5 — State-Driven Design. Prefer snapshot-driven or state-driven reads and rendering when practical. Derive output from a stable state instead of repeatedly rescanning mutable raw collections.
C6 — Performance Discipline. Avoid redundant filter, map, or reduce passes over large datasets when one indexed pass, map, cache, or precomputed snapshot can preserve clarity and correctness. Performance changes require tests and must not alter business meaning.
C7 — Contract Boundary. Programs communicate only through approved APIs, events, SDKs, and Integration Bridge. Direct writes to another program’s database are prohibited.
C8 — Deterministic Integrity. Financial integrity, inventory integrity, idempotency, deduplication, retries, and reconciliation must be deterministic. Silent fallbacks and fabricated defaults are prohibited.
C9 — Tests Before and After. Run baseline tests before editing and all required unit, contract, integration, failure, regression, and reconciliation tests after editing.
C10 — Evidence-Based Completion. Completion requires test evidence, compatibility assessment, resolved documentation impact, deployment and recovery notes, known issues, open risks, and final PASS status. Approval bureaucracy is INACTIVE.


4. Lexical Naming Rules
N1 — Public names use clear English domain terms with one canonical meaning. UI labels, local abbreviations, database column names, and private implementation names do not automatically become public contract names.
N2 — External JSON and SDK field names use lowerCamelCase. Types, schemas, classes, and public model names use PascalCase. Constants use the repository or language convention. A repository may use its language convention internally, but public contract names remain stable.
N3 — Identifiers end with Id, collections use plural nouns, booleans begin with is, has, or can, timestamps end with At, and business dates end with Date when those concepts exist in an approved contract.
N4 — Money names describe the business concept and representation explicitly. A suffix such as AmountMinor is used only when the approved contract defines integer minor units. Currency, quantity, unit, timezone, and date semantics must be explicit.
N5 — Event names follow domain.past-tense-fact.vMajor. Commands use a separate approved contract and may not be disguised as completed facts.
N6 — Examples such as saleId, saleLines, totalAmountMinor, saleStatus, and calculateSaleTotal illustrate naming grammar only. They do not create approved fields, events, statuses, or business rules.


5. Accounting-First Public I/O Baseline
A1 — The first approved public financial contract of the inaugural Accounting program establishes the initial canonical vocabulary for financial input/output names across MAXI.
A2 — Other programs align their public financial integration names at the contract boundary. Their internal names may remain local when an explicit adapter or registered Bridge mapping converts them to the canonical contract.
A3 — Accounting’s internal tables, columns, private classes, and storage schema are implementation details. They are not canonical public I/O and must not be copied into other programs as integration contracts.
A4 — Business applications report approved business facts. They do not send account mappings, debit lines, credit lines, posting decisions, or other journal interpretation owned by Accounting.
A5 — Accounting-first naming applies to financial integration. Inventory remains authoritative for item, stock, costing, and valuation meaning. Integration Bridge remains authoritative for validation, mapping, deduplication, routing, retry, and processing status.
A6 — Missing or conflicting mappings are surfaced explicitly. No AI, application, or Bridge component may guess a mapping from similar names.


6. Contract Governance
G1 — Actual events, commands, fields, types, statuses, mappings, account behavior, tax behavior, timezone, units, validation, and fallback behavior exist only after they are defined in an approved versioned contract.
G2 — Renaming or removing a public field, changing its meaning, requiring a previously optional field, or narrowing an allowed value is a breaking change and requires a major version, RFC, migration plan, compatibility plan, and tests. Affected-owner approval is INACTIVE and advisory.
G3 — Accounting owner, Integration Bridge owner, and affected program owners may provide advisory review. Review is NON-BLOCKING. Bos Cyo's explicit task instruction is the final authority.
G4 — The first approved Accounting contract seeds the canonical financial vocabulary. Future contracts reuse that vocabulary or introduce a versioned, approved replacement. Silent synonyms are prohibited.


7. Inaugural Accounting Workflow
W1 — Before source-code implementation, create the Accounting Module Manifest, Module Context, Current State, relevant Domain Rules, System Glossary entries, public contracts, ADRs, Known Issues, Known Pitfalls, Runbook, Error Catalog, migrations, and affected tests required by scope.

Elle review is advisory and non-blocking.
W3 — Define Accounting domain meaning and public financial capabilities before defining transport fields. Publish versioned schemas and contract tests before other programs implement adapters.
W4 — Other programs integrate through approved SDKs, events, APIs, and Bridge mappings. Existing internal names are adapted at the boundary; mass internal renaming requires its own approved migration.
W5 — An integration is complete only after producer, consumer, compatibility, duplicate, retry, failure, reversal, reconciliation, and recovery tests required by the contract pass.

DOC-IMPACT: REQUIRED — this document establishes the canonical coding and lexical I/O naming standard for the MAXI ecosystem.
Status: ACTIVE. This standard applies immediately to the inaugural Accounting program and every future MAXI program or integration.
No source-code task may be reported complete while required context, contracts, tests, compatibility, documentation, migration recovery, or material ambiguity remains unresolved. Owner/reviewer approval is INACTIVE.


9. SaaS Multi-Tenant Coding and Naming
S1 — Tenant is the canonical term for one subscribing customer company. User identifies a person or service identity; business unit identifies an internal unit belonging to a tenant. These concepts may not be used as synonyms.
S2 — The canonical tenant identifier is tenantId. Silent alternatives such as companyId, organizationId, clientId, or customerCompanyId are prohibited at shared contract boundaries unless a versioned mapping or replacement is explicitly approved.
S3 — Every tenant-owned database row must carry tenantId directly or be provably scoped through an enforced parent relationship. Uniqueness constraints, indexes, idempotency keys, cache keys, object paths, and deduplication records must include tenant scope where required to prevent cross-tenant collision.
S4 — Repository, module, table, field, API, event, job, log, metric, export, backup, and migration naming must make tenant ownership and scope unambiguous.
S5 — Authorization derives the active tenant from verified identity and tenant membership. Code may not trust a tenantId from an unverified request body, query, URL, event, or client state.
S6 — Shared services such as Accounting and Inventory keep one canonical implementation while preserving tenant isolation. Consumer applications do not duplicate their source or tables.
S7 — Tests must cover cross-tenant reads and writes, identifier guessing, duplicate IDs across tenants, cache isolation, event routing, background jobs, exports, imports, backups, recovery, and privileged platform access.
S8 — Names such as tenantMemberships, subscriptionPlan, subscriptionStatus, and moduleEntitlements illustrate naming grammar only. Allowed values and lifecycle behavior require approved contracts and domain rules.
